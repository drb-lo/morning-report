07BlindsideSteelman

How America lost the art of association

Drawing on Tocqueville, Václav Havel and sociologist Robert Nisbet, Luke Burgis argues Americans have let their old genius for forming clubs, congregations and civic associations wither — leaving individuals wired directly into vast online crowds with no mediating institutions, which breeds loneliness and primes a society for conformity and “post-totalitarian” pressure. His prescription is personal: stop waiting for community and build small, accountable associations yourself (Charter 77 had 240 signatories; the Inklings, a handful). It lands with communitarian and traditionalist readers because it reframes the crisis of modern politics as “not too much government, but too little community” — locating social decline in cultural and civic atrophy rather than in policy, and cutting against the progressive instinct to answer atomization mainly through the state.
